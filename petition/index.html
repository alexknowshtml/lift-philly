We are the

Philadelphia's individual business owners are being taxed out of their homes. This is our statement — and our demand for fairness.

We are the people who make Philly work.

We are the barbers, nail techs, estheticians, and tattoo artists who know every face on the block.

We are the food truck vendors, caterers, and pop-up chefs who feed this city.

We are the house cleaners, nannies, and home health aides caring for your children, your parents, and your homes.

We are the contractors, plumbers, electricians, and painters rebuilding your homes.

We are the rideshare drivers and delivery workers keeping the city moving.

We are the graphic designers, writers, journalists, and content creators telling this city's story one project at a time.

We are the photographers, videographers, and social media managers capturing life as it happens.

We are the tutors, personal trainers, event planners, and consultants doing the work that keeps this city running.

We are the musicians, muralists, filmmakers, and performers who make Philly's culture visible.

We are the freelancers, 1099 workers, and sole proprietors — in every neighborhood, in every trade, in every corner of this city.

We are Philly's individual business owners. And we are being taxed out of our homes.

Because of a legal challenge, the City made tax changes impacting nearly 75,000 individual workers and sole proprietors, many working as 1099 contractors. We now face new taxes we simply cannot afford.

For a family business earning $50,000 a year, city taxes have risen by about $1,200 in 2026 and will rise to about $3,200 in 2027 — about $100 to $270 more each month.

That is one month's rent.

That is groceries for a family of four.

That is the heating bill in winter.

That is the AC bill in the summer.

In a city where nearly half of renters already struggle with housing costs, these tax increases don't just take money. They create real harm for families, blocks, and neighborhoods.

The City talks about the unintended consequences of giving us relief. But on every Philly corner and in every neighborhood, cash rules everything around us.

When government pulls cash out of a small business, the harm is immediate:

  • less money for rent
  • less money for groceries
  • less money for childcare
  • less money spent in the neighborhood
  • less stability for the families holding this city together

That is especially true when so many working people are already making less than a living wage.

We are not asking for a handout. We are asking for fairness.

We already pay our share through the Net Profits Tax. But now the City is taxing that same income again under the Business Income and Receipts Tax. That means many individual business owners face a double tax — and can end up paying a higher rate than corporations.

We are not giant corporations.

We are working families trying to survive.

The biggest unintended consequence is already here: neighborhood businesses losing cash, families losing stability, and local communities losing the dollars that keep them alive.

The real unintended consequence is not that a small number of outlier millionaires might get a tax break. That risk is already limited — and even smaller than some suggest — because Philadelphia already gives BIRT relief to manufacturers, wholesalers, and retailers through the alternative method. The number of businesses that could create the worst-case unintended consequence is much smaller than it first appears.

The real unintended consequence is that thousands of working Philadelphians will have money pulled out of their neighborhoods, weakening the local businesses that keep those neighborhoods going.

The real unintended consequence is that the City may spend tens of millions of dollars on programs and fixes that can help less than 10 percent of affected businesses — while Councilmember Driscoll's structural tax solution could help far more people, more fairly, and more efficiently.

We need a simple, fair, and responsible solution.

On November 20, 2025, Councilmember Driscoll introduced Bill No. 251026. On March 25, 2026, the Law Department confirmed this solution is constitutional. We are calling on City Council to pass Bill No. 251026 and create a new tax class and BIRT income exemption for Individual Sole Proprietors and Single-Member LLCs whose sole member is an Individual.

This solution would:

  • end the complex double tax on individuals that leaves them paying a higher rate than corporations
  • reduce unnecessary compliance costs for both the City and our individual business owners
  • protect working families while keeping local dollars in our communities
  • strengthen neighborhood economies instead of draining them

This is about more than tax policy. It is about fairness. It is about family stability. It is about whether Philadelphia wants to be a city where working people can still afford to live, build, and stay.

We Are Philly.

We are your neighbors.

We are your customers.

We are your community.

We are the heart of this city.

And we pledge to stand together against the unintended consequences of taxing working people out of their neighborhoods.

We ask the Administration, City Council, and community partners to come together and pass this reform now.

When our individual-owned businesses thrive, Philadelphia thrives.

We Are Philly, and we deserve a fair chance to stay, work, and build here.
