# Campaign Advocate Review — LIFT Philly

## Overall Assessment

Speaking as a Philly independent worker who heard about this site and wants to know if I should sign: the site earns my trust, but it doesn't give me a clear moment to act. I landed on the homepage, read about the problem, got genuinely angry, scrolled to the bottom looking for "where do I sign?" — and found a calculator, a PDF, and a share button. I had to go back to the nav to find the petition. That's a conversion fail. The petition page itself, once I found it, is excellent — compelling, respectful, specific. But finding it requires initiative I might not have had on a busy day.

---

## What Works — Don't Change These

**The rotating wheel on the petition hero.** Seeing "barbers who know every face on the block" rotating through professions is the most effective element on the entire site. It creates instant identification. If I'm a barber, I see myself. The personal specificity ("know every face on the block") makes it real, not generic. 

**The Council quotes.** Having Councilmembers Driscoll, Thomas, and Brooks quoted is critical credibility. These aren't activists — these are elected officials sounding alarms. The Councilman Thomas video link ("Watch full quote — 1:02:31") is a strong trust signal.

**The tax rate comparison on the petition page.** Corporation pays 5.71%. The laid-off worker pays 6.024%. That visual is the clearest distillation of the injustice. It's on the petition page but should be on the homepage.

**The calculator.** Seeing my specific number ($2,400+) is personalizing and motivating. Once I've done the calculation, I'm ready to act.

**The petition text itself.** "The work has been done in good faith. Now faith must become action." This is good. The acknowledgment of Councilmembers by name shows this campaign has done the work and has relationships. That signals legitimacy and that signing will actually matter.

---

## What Creates Hesitation

### "What exactly am I signing?"

CAUTION: The petition title is "Sign the Petition, Request a Hearing." But the petition header badge says "Petition for Hearing." Not for the legislation to pass — for a hearing. 

As a prospective signer, I want to know: what specifically happens when I sign? Does my name go to City Council? Is there a public hearing being demanded? What does the council see? A one-sentence explanation near the form of exactly what happens with signatures would reduce hesitation: "Your signature joins the list submitted to City Council as we request a formal hearing on Bill No. 251026."

### "Is this real / is it secure?"

NOTE: The petition form has a "Not shown publicly" note under Email and "not shown publicly" under Zip. This is good. But there's no broader statement about data use. Add: "We only use your info to count signatures and notify you of hearing dates. We never sell or share your data."

### "Does signing actually matter?"

CAUTION: The petition doesn't explain the path to impact. "We ask City Council to hold a hearing and bring Bill No. 251026 to a vote" is clear — but what happens after signatures are collected? Is there a threshold? Will they be delivered in person? At a hearing? The more concrete the impact path, the more motivated signers feel. Even: "When we reach [N] signatures, we deliver this petition in person at City Hall" would help.

### "Will I be publicly embarrassed or targeted?"

NOTE: The "anonymous" checkbox helps address this concern for people who are worried about visibility. But it's styled small at the bottom of the form and easy to miss. For workers in industries with employer-client relationships (freelancers, contractors) who worry about blowback, the anonymous option is important. Consider adding a note near the name field: "Prefer not to use your real name? You can sign anonymously below." This surfaces the option at the point of hesitation rather than burying it at the bottom.

### "Am I affected?"

CAUTION: The homepage says "75,000" businesses affected. The petition page says "nearly 75,000." These numbers don't match and create confusion. If I read the homepage first (75K) then the petition (75K), I wonder if I'm reading about the same campaign. Pick one number and use it consistently, or explain the difference (e.g., "75,000 sole proprietors and 47,000 other individual entities").

### "Is this still happening?"

NOTE: The calculator has a "Last updated: Jan 17, 2026" badge. The filing-your-birt page has a letter dated "January 15, 2026." It is now April 2026 — the tax filing deadline. Is this still relevant? Has the hearing happened? Has anything changed? A visitor in April 2026 doesn't know if this campaign is still active or if the bill passed/failed. A sticky banner or "Campaign Status" element on the homepage showing current status would prevent "is this still happening?" churn.

## What's Missing for Conversion

### No "why now" urgency

The homepage says the tax increase hits "in 2026" — but it's April 2026, which means the filing deadline is right now or just passed. The copy isn't updated to reflect the immediacy. For an April 2026 visitor, this should read: "The April 15 filing deadline just hit. Here's what you're about to pay." The urgency is maximal right now and the site doesn't use it.

### No specific next step after petition

After signing, what do I do? The success state offers LinkedIn sharing — good — but doesn't tell me what happens next. "Next: We notify your council representative of your signature" or "Next: Watch for our email when the hearing is scheduled" would close the loop and make me feel like signing did something real.

### No testimonials from actual affected workers

The site quotes councilmembers and officials. It doesn't quote individual affected workers — the barbers, the food truck owners, the freelancers. A single testimonial ("I've run my nail salon for 8 years. This tax would cost me 2 months of groceries." — Maria S., North Philly) would humanize the campaign for people who identify with the worker rather than the policy.

### Discrepancy in call to action context

The petition page has `noindex` set in the meta. If this page isn't ready for general public traffic, it shouldn't be linked from the nav as "Sign the Petition" — that sends traffic to a page the campaign itself is hiding from search engines. Either commit to the page (remove noindex) or explain why it's restricted.

---

## Priority Hesitation Removers

1. **Homepage → add petition CTA in body.** Someone who wants to sign and hits the homepage can't find the petition without going back to the nav. This is the #1 drop-off moment.

2. **Consistent affected-person count.** 75K vs 75K creates doubt. Unify.

3. **Add campaign status indicator.** "The Finance Committee hearing has not yet been scheduled. That's why your signature matters." Real-time status = real-time relevance.

4. **Surface anonymous option earlier.** Many affected workers (contractors, freelancers with clients) worry about visibility. The anonymous checkbox is buried; surfacing it reduces hesitation for a meaningful segment.

5. **Add a "what happens with my signature" sentence near the form.** The most common reason people don't sign petitions is that they don't believe it will matter. Tell them exactly what happens.

6. **Update time-sensitive language.** "2026" as a future event is no longer accurate — we are in 2026, and for some it's already past April 15. Update body copy to reflect current timing.
