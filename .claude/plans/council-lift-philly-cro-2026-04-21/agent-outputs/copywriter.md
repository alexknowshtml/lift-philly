# Copywriter Review — LIFT Philly

## Overall Assessment

The petition page has some of the strongest campaign copy I've seen for a local civic issue. The rotating wheel of worker identities, the "We Are Philly" closing, and the "Cash Rules Everything Around Us" section are genuinely effective. The problem is that this emotional quality is almost entirely confined to the petition page — the homepage reads like an advocacy organization briefing document, not a call to action. The copy does not drive visitors toward signing anything.

---

## Homepage Copy Audit

### Hero Headline

Current: **"Protecting Philly's economic wellbeing"**

CAUTION: This headline is passive and organizational. It sounds like a nonprofit's mission statement, not a call to action. It describes what LIFT Philly is doing rather than what's happening to the reader. Compare to the petition page headline which puts the reader at the center ("We are the barbers...").

Suggested direction: Lead with the threat or the identity of those affected.
- "75,000 Philly workers are about to pay more taxes than Comcast."
- "Your tax bill is going up. We're fighting to stop it."
- "Philadelphia is taxing its own workers out of the city."

### Hero Subtitle

Current: **"75,000 working families, tradespeople, caregivers, gig workers, and creative entrepreneurs are facing a massive tax increase in 2026 and beyond."**

NOTE: This is solid — specific, human, present-tense urgency. But "2026 and beyond" at the end deflates the urgency slightly. The tax is already happening (April 2026 filing deadline). Tighten to: "75,000 Philly workers are facing a surprise tax bill this April."

### Hero Signup Context

The LIFT acronym explanation block above the email form is well-intentioned but adds cognitive load before the ask. A visitor reading the hero for the first time is being asked to understand an acronym before they act. The acronym explanation could move to the footer or an "About" section.

**CRITICAL: The hero email form has no petition mention**

The email form captures the most engaged visitors at their highest attention moment. There is no reference to the petition anywhere in or around the hero. A single line beneath the email form — "Already ready to act? Sign the petition →" — would redirect petition-motivated visitors without disrupting email signups.

### Problem Section Headlines

Current: **"Philadelphia's tax code wasn't built for the modern workforce"**

NOTE: Accurate but analytical. "Tax code" frames this as a policy discussion. More visceral: "Philadelphia is charging you more than it charges Comcast." Or lean into the timeline: "Starting this April, your tax bill changes forever."

The Councilman Thomas quote ("I don't think we realize how catastrophic this is gonna be") is excellent and should be higher — ideally near the top of the problem section rather than buried in a dark card.

### Solution Section

Current button/label text in timeline: **"Bill introduced by Councilman Driscoll / Nov 25"**

NOTE: The timeline shows "TBD" for the Finance Committee Hearing. This is an opportunity to frame urgency: "Hearing not yet scheduled — that's why we need your voice." Adding a line of copy beneath the timeline pointing to the petition would convert the solution section into a funnel entry point.

### Resources Section

Current button: **"Calculate Your Tax Impact"** (primary, navy)

The Resources section is named "Learn More & Get Involved" but the buttons are: Calculator, One-Pager, Sign Up to Testify, Share. "Sign the Petition" is absent.

CRITICAL: "Sign Up to Testify" (Tally link) appears here but the petition does not. Testifying is a higher-friction ask than signing a petition. Someone willing to testify will absolutely sign — but someone who sees "testify" and feels unqualified will not click it. The petition is the lower-friction gateway that should appear first.

### Footer

Current: Brand name + descriptor line + legal disclaimer

NOTE: No calls to action, no links to petition or join form. Every footer should have at minimum: "Sign the Petition | Join the Coalition | liftphilly.org"

## Filing Your BIRT Copy Audit

### Page Header

Current: **"Filing Your BIRT: Official City Guidance"**
Subtitle: **"Essential information for every Philadelphia business owner"**

NOTE: The page is presented as information-only. But the audience — people who just learned they have to file BIRT for the first time — is exactly who the petition is designed for. The header could add a banner or intro line: "This is what you're up against. Here's what we're doing about it." Then link to the petition.

### CTA Section at Bottom

Current: **"Support the LIFT Act" → "Join the Coalition →"**

CAUTION: "Join the Coalition" is a weak CTA for someone who just read that they have to pay $1,200–$3,700 more in taxes starting now. This is the moment of maximum anger and motivation. The CTA should be: "Sign the Petition" with "Join the Coalition" as secondary.

Suggested:
- Primary: "Add your name to the petition →" (gold button, links to /petition)
- Secondary: "Get updates from the coalition" (outline button, links to /#join)

## Calculator Copy Audit

### Hero

Current: **"Know Before You File"**
Subtitle: **"See how the removal of the $100K BIRT exemption will affect your Philadelphia individual business starting in 2026."**

NOTE: Functional but not motivating. After a user calculates their impact, nothing in the copy bridges from "here's your number" to "here's what you can do about it."

### Post-Result "Estimated Quote" Section

Current heading: **"Wait, what are estimated payments?"**
Current CTA: "Join the Coalition" button

CAUTION: The post-result section explains estimated payments, which is useful. But the CTA pivot from "here's your money problem" to "join our email list" is too soft. After explaining the cash shock, the copy should say something like:

"This isn't inevitable. The LIFT Act would fix it — but City Council needs to hear from you."
→ [Sign the Petition] [Join the Coalition]

### Bottom CTA Section

Current: **"Ready to Take Action? Join the coalition fighting for fair tax treatment..."**

CAUTION: "Fair tax treatment" is bureaucratic language. After showing someone they'll owe $3,700 more this April, the copy should match the emotional register: "You just saw what this tax does to your business. Now do something about it."

## Petition Page Copy Audit

### Hero

The rotating wheel of professions is excellent. Strong emotional hook. The phrases are vivid and specific ("barbers who know every face on the block," "contractors & plumbers who rebuilt this city one house at a time"). This works.

CAUTION: The badge reads "Petition for Hearing" — this is accurate but not emotionally priming. Consider "It's Time to Vote" or "City Council Must Act" as a badge that frames urgency.

### Petition Body

Strong overall. Key moments:

- **CRITICAL:** "We do not blame City Council for eliminating the $100,000 BIRT exemption. You believed you had no choice." — This is generous and strategically smart for political persuasion, but it may undermine the emotional urgency for a reader deciding whether to sign. It walks back the outrage just as it's building.

- The "Cash Rules Everything Around Us" section with the dark card is bold and works on Philly-natives but may read as obscure to less-engaged visitors. No change needed but worth noting.

- "The work has been done in good faith. Now faith must become action." — This is excellent.

- "Our Ask" box copy is clear and specific. Good.

### Form Section

Heading: **"Add Your Name"**

NOTE: Mild. "Sign the Petition" is stronger — it's an action, not a description. The subhead "Sign this petition and stand with Philadelphia's working business owners" is fine but "stand with" is overused. "Add your voice to 247 others" (with real count) is more effective.

### Success State

Current: LinkedIn share prompt with suggested text.

CAUTION: The suggested LinkedIn text is solid. But the success screen offers no path to join the coalition email list. These are the most motivated users on the site. They just signed a petition. They are warm. Ask for the second action: "Want updates on the vote? Join the coalition." A single line and link would capture this.

Also: "Your signature has been received. It will appear below once reviewed." — "once reviewed" introduces uncertainty. Does this mean it might be rejected? Soften to "once approved, usually within 24 hours" or simply "it will appear below shortly."
